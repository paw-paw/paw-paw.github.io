For a few years, esports grew with a logic of acceleration: more tournaments, more streams, more investment, and bigger expectations. The pandemic pushed that digital dynamic even further. After that, the industry started correcting. Interest did not disappear, but the models had to become more sustainable and prove real value for brands and communities.

## Returning to physical spaces

In that context, bringing esports back into physical spaces should be understood as a way to rebuild connection. We do not always need a stadium to do that. Sometimes, what the community needs is an accessible place where people can meet. That is where malls start to make sense.

A mall is not just a venue with foot traffic. It is a social space: people go there to eat, shop, walk, watch a movie, meet friends, or discover something they were not actively looking for. For a gaming community, the mall can become more than a rented space. It can become a meeting point.

> A stadium can make esports feel big. A mall can make esports feel close.

## What the mall makes visible

An in-person final at a mall does something online formats do not always achieve: it makes the community visible. A streamed tournament mostly speaks to people who are already inside the scene. A mall event can also reach families, casual visitors, brands, media, and people who may not follow esports but can understand the energy of a live competition.

That was part of the value of the pc Factory Streamers Cup, which we produced from XP Agencia. The project brought together leading Dota 2 streamers, community participation through open qualifiers, online competition, and a live grand final at Mall Aventura Santa Anita.

## More than a logistical option

For us, the mall was more than a practical venue choice. It connected digital reach, in-person attendance, retail, sponsors, creators, and community in the same experience.

A mall will not replace a dedicated arena. It plays a different role. It can make gaming feel closer, more visible, and more connected to everyday life. For technology, retail, consumer, or entertainment brands, that can be more valuable than simply appearing on a stream.

## Why proximity matters

A stadium can make esports feel big. A mall can make esports feel close.

At a time when the industry needs more sustainable growth, that proximity matters. Esports do not only need more screens. They need places where the community can be seen, gather, and build real experiences around the game.
