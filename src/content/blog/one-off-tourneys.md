One-off tournaments can work. They create attention, give players a reason to participate, and help brands enter gaming culture without overcommitting. But they become weak when they are treated as the whole strategy.

## Where tournaments fall short

Gaming is no longer an experimental space. Newzoo estimates the global games market at $188.8B in 2025, with around 3.6B players worldwide. At the same time, growth is becoming steadier and less explosive, which means brands can no longer rely on novelty alone. Showing up in gaming is not enough anymore.

That is where isolated tournaments fall short. A single event gives a brand a few moments of exposure: announcement, registration, match day, winner post, recap. Then the conversation usually ends. The brand was seen, but not necessarily remembered.

> One-off tournaments create moments. Programs create value.

## Why programs work

A program creates more value because it builds repeated touchpoints. Players return. Stories develop. Creators have more to talk about. Communities have a reason to stay active. The brand becomes part of the experience instead of just appearing around it.

This matters because commercial value in esports is not built only through exposure. A [2025 study](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1594068/full) found that sponsor-esports congruence plays an important role in connecting esports consumption with sponsor brand equity, including brand awareness, brand image, and brand loyalty. In simpler terms: the audience needs to feel that the brand makes sense in the space.

## What brands should build

A stronger esports program can achieve that.

A one-off tournament can show that a brand is interested in gamers. A well-designed esports program can show what the brand actually contributes: access, competition, entertainment, recognition, community, or opportunity.

This does not mean every brand needs to build a massive league. A one-off tournament can be a useful pilot. It can test a game, a format, a community, or a sponsor fit. But without a next step, most of the value fades right after the final match.

## The better question

I do not think brands should be asking themselves: "Should we run a tournament?"

Rather, a better question is: "What relationship do we want to build with this audience after the tournament ends?"

One-off tournaments create moments. Esports programs create value.
